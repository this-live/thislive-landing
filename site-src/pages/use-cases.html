Lead follow-up

Answer every inquiry while it is still warm

Most small businesses lose work to slow replies, not bad prices. An assistant watches your website form and inbox, drafts a reply using your services, prices and calendar, and sends it to you for a one-tap approval. Approved replies go out, the contact is logged and a follow-up is scheduled.

Good fit if

  • {{icon:check}}Inquiries sit for a day or more
  • {{icon:check}}The same questions come in every week
  • {{icon:check}}Follow-ups depend on someone remembering

What you get

  • {{icon:check}}Drafted replies in your voice
  • {{icon:check}}Approval from your phone
  • {{icon:check}}Contacts and history in your CRM

Paperwork to data

Stop retyping invoices and forms

Invoices, intake forms, receipts and work orders arrive as PDFs and photos. The build reads each one, pulls out the fields you care about, checks them against your rules and enters them where they belong. Anything that does not add up waits for a person instead of slipping through.

Good fit if

  • {{icon:check}}Someone types documents into a system
  • {{icon:check}}Typos and duplicates cost you money
  • {{icon:check}}Paperwork piles up at month end

What you get

  • {{icon:check}}Fields read off each document
  • {{icon:check}}Totals re-checked automatically
  • {{icon:check}}A review queue for the odd ones

Weekly report

Get the numbers without building the spreadsheet

The report someone rebuilds every Monday pulls from the same places every time. The build collects the numbers from your sales, jobs and accounting tools, assembles the report in the format you already read and adds short notes on what changed. It lands in your inbox before the workday starts.

Good fit if

  • {{icon:check}}A report takes hours to assemble
  • {{icon:check}}Numbers live in three or more tools
  • {{icon:check}}You find problems after the fact

What you get

  • {{icon:check}}A report on a schedule you set
  • {{icon:check}}Notes on what went up and down
  • {{icon:check}}Alerts when something needs attention

Spreadsheet to app

Turn the spreadsheet only one person understands into a tool

Many businesses run on one master spreadsheet and the one person who knows its rules. The build keeps what works, moves the data into a real database with validation and history, and gives each person a simple screen for their job, on any device. A live dashboard replaces the weekly copy and paste.

Good fit if

  • {{icon:check}}One file runs the business
  • {{icon:check}}People overwrite each other's work
  • {{icon:check}}The team works from phones in the field

What you get

  • {{icon:check}}A database with rules built in
  • {{icon:check}}Screens for each role
  • {{icon:check}}A live dashboard

Real build: Storrs Aquatics

A season of swim practices, planned and checked

Swim coaches write practices for several training groups, every day, all season. I built a planning engine for the coaches at Storrs Aquatics: it takes the season calendar, the groups and the club's rules, writes each practice to the right distance and focus, and runs every practice through a rule checker before printing sheets for the pool deck.

The same pattern fits any business that produces schedules, plans or quotes from rules a person currently keeps in their head.

Built with

  • {{icon:check}}A planning engine with the club's rules
  • {{icon:check}}An automatic rule checker
  • {{icon:check}}Printable sheets and group cards

Same pattern for

  • {{icon:check}}Crew and shift scheduling
  • {{icon:check}}Quotes built from pricing rules
  • {{icon:check}}Production and job planning
