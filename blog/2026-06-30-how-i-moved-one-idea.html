How I moved one idea through every surface I own

June 30, 2026

In one week in mid-June I changed the same claim in six separate passes across the pages of my own site, and the most useful of those passes changed a single line.

Why I made the edits

I had told visitors that the first adapters for Forge were in training, and nothing was actually training. I had written the line myself, so I assumed it was true, and it sat there in nine places until I finally read my own site the way a stranger would. A landing page is the one piece of writing you cannot edit out, so a false line there is a promise I had to keep re-reading. I wanted the site to stop describing work that did not exist.

The tools I was editing through

My landing page is the face my site shows the internet, so that is where I started. The Forge page is the part of my stack meant to fine-tune small models on my own writing, and that is where the honest fix landed. My resume page is the short answer I give when someone asks what I build, and it carried the same false line, fixed in a single line. The philosophy page is where I write down why, and it got a full rewrite. The six product cards on the home page describe each tool, and they picked up new words and their own animations in the same pass.

What I learned

I wrote a false state onto a public page and let it sit for days, assuming it was true simply because I had typed it. I was the blocker, not the system: the information was right in front of me, and I only moved it when I looked at every surface at the same time. The honest version of the week is one short line that finally told the truth about work that had not started.

What I did not fix

I only changed the pages I remembered to check. I do not have any tool that warns me when two of my pages disagree, so the next drift will be found the same way, by hand.

If I open every page together once more, the site will at least tell the truth while I am looking.
