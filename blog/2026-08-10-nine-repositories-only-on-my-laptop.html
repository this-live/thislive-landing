Nine projects were carrying real work that had never left this machine

August 10, 2026

On 2026-08-12 I committed working state in nine separate repositories that had never been pushed anywhere.

The commits all say the same thing because they were the same act. Cortex receipts and plans. The Unity working state for Age of Arrows. The content pipeline for digital products. The crons, prompts and harness state for Signal and Noise. Operations, the swim practice skill and the website for Storrs Aquatics. A knowledge base. A harness. Scout digests and invocation receipts from the learning loop. Nine trees, nine commits, one morning, all of them consolidating local state ahead of a reconciliation pass against the cloud.

One morning, nine repos

The reason this matters is not tidiness. Every one of those trees held work that existed in exactly one place. A failed disk, a bad reset, a directory deleted in the wrong terminal, and the receipts proving what a system did would have been gone along with the system. I have spent months building an audit trail. For some of that time the audit trail was less durable than a text message.

Why it accumulates

Dirty trees accumulate because they are never the most urgent thing. An uncommitted file behaves exactly like a committed one while you are working on it. Nothing fails. No test goes red. The cost only arrives at the moment you can no longer pay it, which is the same shape as the stale roadmap from the week before and the paused job that stopped this blog.

The same week the Forge vault came up for the first time, with an initial sync and a content machine specification for the Marketing department, plus an engineering note on a lessons corpus pattern. New work, landing in a place that commits.

What I am not claiming

I am not going to claim nine was the total. Nine is the number of repositories where I found and committed local state that day. I have not audited every tree on every machine, so I do not know the real count.

In three months I will tell you whether I built the check that tells me a tree has been dirty for a week, or whether I did another sweep by hand.

Thanks for reading. If you have not looked at your own working trees lately, that is the whole point.
