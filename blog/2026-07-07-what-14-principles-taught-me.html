What 14 principles taught me about operating as one person

July 7, 2026

This week I shipped the same view three times, and the only principle that really shaped the work was the one that says do not report a success you cannot prove.

Why that rule won

It mattered this week because the page that shows my own project list hung the background service behind it. The page looked right, so it was reporting a success, and the whole service died. I rebuilt that page twice before I stopped trying to fix it in place. The third rebuild ran the refresh in a separate worker process, so a hang could only take down one small part instead of the whole thing.

What I was running

On my phone I now have an Android shell for the app. It has no app code and no credentials of its own. I scanned the built package for 22 of my own secrets and found none. My voice runs through a relay in the gateway, which attaches the access token on the server side and strips any token the phone sends. A tokenless call through the relay went through. The same call aimed straight at the service was refused, which was the point. By the end of the week the voice could also run against Bob, the computer in my house with the graphics card, instead of a hosted service.

What I got wrong

The honest version is that the cut itself taught me less than I claimed. I made that cut in mid-June, and shaving five principles off the list was an afternoon of editing. Figuring out which of the nine were real took the next three weeks of shipping, and the shipping is what proved which ones mattered. The work I did this week is the evidence. The principle on the page was not.

What I cannot prove yet

I cannot tell you which of the nine I actually used this week, because that needs reading my own commit log afterward. That makes them a document I agree with, not a set of principles I operate by. I do not yet know the difference, and I would not pretend otherwise.

In a few weeks I will write which of the nine showed up, and I will not call the nine real until I can name them from the work, not the list.
