The receipt convention keeps the work honest

June 22, 2026

This week I made my two coding agents write a receipt for every change they make.

Why I wanted this

I hand a task to a coding agent and wait for it to come back, and in between I was just trusting my memory. A receipt is a short note the job writes after it finishes, saying what it changed and what it expected. I started requiring one every time anything changed. Codex and Cursor, the two agents I route work to, both finished the round trip, and their receipts came back with real token counts and exit codes instead of a guess.

The tools, plainly

I added a route that lists those job sessions with a stable status, so I can see what ran. One receipt field told whether a job met its acceptance criteria, but it returned a value in the wrong shape, so I fixed it. The part I had not planned for was that I could not tell which version of the code each service was running, so three services now stamp their health output with the version, and one of them self-tests that stamp at boot. A gate now samples three of those note writers against the one expected shape instead of trusting them to agree.

What I got wrong

I had been reading the health output of my services as if it told me which version was live. It did not. Before I added the version stamp, I genuinely could not answer which commit was running on my own machines, and I was acting like the health check had told me. The honest version is that the stamp had to be built, because it was not there. I was the blocker, not the system.

The honest limit

A receipt only records what the writer chose to put in it. The gate I added samples three of the writers, not all of them, and nothing yet tells me when a job that should have written a receipt wrote nothing at all. I have not solved the gap between what got recorded and what should have been.

I will write an honest update when I close the empty-receipt gap.
